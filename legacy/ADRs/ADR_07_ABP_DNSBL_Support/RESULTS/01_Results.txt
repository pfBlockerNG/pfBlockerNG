VERDICT (headline): GO (with mitigations). Full ABP DNS support is safe/cheap
enough to build. The irreducible-regex per-query cost is ~0.16 us/pattern on a
negative query, so the ~50 us added-latency budget bounds to ~300 irreducible

patterns fold to dicts (measured 46% reduction on a metachar-heavy corpus; real

thread/asyncio timeout CANNOT cancel a runaway match — which is exactly why the
design accepts one slow first-hit then EVICTS, rather than vetting.

3. MEASUREMENTS  (CPython 3.11.15, linux; spike_adr07_regex.py)
================================================================================

--- 3a. REGEX REDUCTION RATIO -------------------------------------------------
Reducible grammar (folds to a domain/wildcard rule, ZERO per-query cost):
    ^(.+\.)?D$   |  (^|\.)D$   |  ^(?:.+\.)?D$   ->  wildcard ZONE (D + subs)
    ^D$          |  ^(www\.)?D$  (folded to D)  ->  exact DATA
  where D = labels of [a-z0-9-] joined by ESCAPED dots (\.), no other metachar.
  Anything with a char-class, label quantifier, body alternation, or floating
  dot is IRREDUCIBLE (kept as a compiled pattern). Per ADR §2 we DO NOT expand
  finite classes (ad[0-9]\.x stays a regex).
MEASURED: 39 total /re/+@@/re/  ->  18 reducible (46%)  /  21 irreducible.
  Reduced examples: /^(.+\.)?doubleclick\.net$/ -> ('zone','doubleclick.net');
  /^pixel\.example\.com$/ -> ('data','pixel.example.com'); @@ forms fold to a
  whiteDB wildcard/exact entry.

--- 3b. IRREDUCIBLE COUNT + PER-QUERY LATENCY (the ADR-01-class risk) ----------
Inline O(n) compiled-scan, NEGATIVE query (worst case — scanned against EVERY
pattern; positive queries short-circuit on first hit). tracemalloc OFF.
  irreducible count   added us/query   us/pattern
            10              1.88          0.188
            50              8.37          0.167
           100             16.6           0.166
           200             33.8           0.169
           500             81.2           0.162
          1000            160.6           0.161
  => ~0.16 us per irreducible pattern (linear, negative query). Against a ~50 us
     added-latency budget this bounds to ~310 irreducible patterns. Positive
     queries are cheaper (first-hit break). The dict baseline is ~1 us/query
     (ADR-05 §3a) and is UNCHANGED — regex only runs when data/zone/tld/idn all
     miss (evaluate_domain :2312 gate), i.e. mostly on negative/non-blocked names.

--- 3c. ReDoS EXPOSURE + worst real first-hit ---------------------------------
Static heuristic (the opt-in "Limit long/complex regex" cap candidate, cheap, NO
execution): flag if pattern length > 200 OR a NESTED QUANTIFIER
(regex \([^()]*[+*][^()]*\)\s*[+*{] — a quantified group inside a quantifier:
(a+)+, (a*)*, (\w+\.)+, ([a-z]+)*, (.*a){20}).
MEASURED on the 21 irreducible patterns:
  * 4 statically flagged (all nested-quantifier): (a+)+$, (\w+\.)+\w+$,
    ([a-z]+)*\.example$, (.*a){20}$.
  * Worst real first-hit on a <=253-char input (timed in a KILLABLE subprocess,
    2 s hard cap):
        ([a-z]+)*\.example$   >= 2000 ms  (TIMED OUT/killed)   [flagged]
        (.*a){20}$               ~44 ms                        [flagged]
        [a-z0-9]{8,}\.cloudfront-ads\.example$  ~0.14 ms       (cap-passing)
        ... all other cap-passing patterns  < 0.05 ms
  * Pathological (>= 2 s) patterns: 1 caught by the static cap, 0 MISSED.
  * WORST CAP-PASSING first-hit: ~0.14 ms. i.e. once the static cap drops the
    nested-quantifier shapes, NO surviving pattern in the corpus runs slow enough
    to matter for a single first-hit.

--- 3d. GIL DEMO — a thread/asyncio timeout CANNOT interrupt a runaway match ---
Worker thread runs (a+)+$ on a failing ~23-char input; a waiter calls
join(timeout=1 ms). MEASURED: the waiter was forced to wait ~270 ms (~270x its
1 ms deadline); the match ran to completion UNCANCELLED; there is no API to kill
the worker. CONFIRMS ADR fact 2: a query-time thread/asyncio timeout is futile
under the GIL — the C-level re.search holds the GIL so the waiter can't even wake
to honour the deadline, and the runaway worker can't be cancelled. THIS is WHY
the design accepts a single slow first-hit then EVICTS, rather than vetting or a
query-time timeout. (re2 would give linear-time matching and dodge this, but it
is a non-stdlib compiled dependency -> out of scope, ADR §2.)

--- 3e. runtime-safety mechanism CONFIRMED ------------------------------------
* time.thread_time() IS available on this CPython (and on FreeBSD/pfSense it maps
  to CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID — present). Use it for per-match thread-CPU timing
  (jitter-robust vs wall clock: a descheduled thread can't false-evict a good
  pattern). FALLBACK if a target lacks it: time.perf_counter() + a 2-strike guard
  (don't evict on a single wall-clock spike). Verify on the live box in Phase 9.
* EVICTION must snapshot-iterate + evict-AFTER-loop (never mutate regexDB/
  allowRegexDB mid-iteration); under the GIL a dict.pop is atomic, so collect the
  keys to evict during the scan and pop them after the for-loop. Applies to feed
  AND user regex.
* STATIC CAP heuristic (opt-in): length cap + nested-quantifier detection (the
  exact regex above caught 1/1 catastrophic, 0 false-negatives, 0 false-positives
  on the corpus's cap-passing set). Drop over-cap patterns at LOAD (no execution).

4. KILL-THRESHOLD + GATE  (proposed; TUNE WITH MAINTAINER)
================================================================================
Proposed kill-threshold:
  (a) Added MEDIAN per-query latency from the inline irreducible scan stays "a
      few us" at the realistic irreducible count, with a 50 us hard ceiling;
      bounded by the reduction ratio. Per-pattern ~0.16 us => >=300 irreducible
      patterns fit the 50 us ceiling.
  (b) Reduction ratio high enough to keep the irreducible count well under (a):
      floor 30% on the metachar-heavy corpus (real feeds reduce higher).
  (c) Worst CAP-PASSING first-hit on a <=253-char input is sub-second (proposed
      evict ceiling 100 ms thread-CPU; warn 10 ms). Measured worst cap-passing
      ~0.14 ms — three orders of magnitude under the evict ceiling.

GATE RESULT (spike, default corpus):
  reduction ratio          46%  >= 30%                 : PASS
  per-pattern cost   0.16 us  (>=300 fit 50 us budget) : PASS
  worst cap-passing first-hit  ~0.14 ms <= 500 ms      : PASS
  VERDICT: GO (with mitigations)

PROPOSED CEILINGS (confirm/tune with maintainer; ADR §2 defaults):
  warn  = 10 ms  thread-CPU per match  (log a warning)
  evict = 100 ms thread-CPU per match  (log error + remove pattern from live DB)
  static cap (opt-in): length > ~200 chars OR nested-quantifier -> drop at load.
  All configurable (advanced settings, Phase 7).

================================================================================
5. GO / PIVOT DECISION FOR PHASE 2
================================================================================
GO — proceed to Phase 2 (ABP DNS decision spec + golden oracle) and build the
full ABP path. Rationale:
  * The two front-loaded risks are bounded by measurement: irreducible regex is
    ~0.16 us/pattern (budget bounds to ~300 patterns; reduction + the
    important_rules fast path keep typical deployments far under that), and the
    only genuinely catastrophic shapes are nested-quantifier patterns the cheap
    static cap catches with no execution and no false-negatives on the corpus.
  * The GIL demo confirms the residual (one slow first-hit) is irreducible
    without a subprocess/re2 — both out of scope — so the accept-then-evict design
    is the right minimum-effort mitigation, not a shortcut.
  * No reject-criterion (ADR §7) was tripped.

PIVOTS HELD IN RESERVE (record if a later phase or a real-feed re-measurement in
Phase 9 trips a threshold — ADR §7):
  * If real-feed irreducible counts blow the latency budget: translate-only (fold
    reducible regex into dicts, DROP irreducible), or force the opt-in static cap
    ON by default.
  * If real feeds carry patterns that hang long enough to matter even with warn/
    evict AND the cap can't catch them cheaply: translate-only.
  * If sovereignty/precedence can't be reproduced on the Phase-2 spec: STOP and
    reconcile before deleting the PHP lite pass.
